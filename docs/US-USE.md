# US use

Last updated 24 September 2026 by the project maintainers.
Supplements [Intended use](INTENDED-USE.md). This is deployment guidance, not an
FDA determination, privacy notice or complete state-law assessment.

## Functions and claims

Operators must keep functionality, interfaces and promotion consistent with
[intended use](INTENDED-USE.md#purpose-and-limits). A disclaimer cannot correct
contradictory functionality or marketing.

Some general-wellness software is excluded from the device definition (FD&C Act
section 520(o)(1)(B)); other low-risk wellness products fall within enforcement
discretion under FDA’s
[general-wellness guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/general-wellness-policy-low-risk-devices).
Neither is FDA clearance or approval.

Do not claim clinical accuracy, FDA approval/clearance, HIPAA compliance or
anonymity without an applicable basis. Substantiate health and performance claims,
including demonstrations and endorsements; explain the tested scope. Display the
[intended-use notice](INTENDED-USE.md#purpose-and-limits) alongside outputs.

## Privacy and incidents

- **HIPAA:** assess whether the operator is a covered entity or business associate.
  Consumer wellness may fall outside HIPAA; handling data for a covered provider
  may create different obligations. Where applicable, implement required safeguards
  and business-associate arrangements. See [HHS guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/covered-entities/index.html).
- **FTC:** assess the [Health Breach Notification Rule](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-318)
  for qualifying personal-health-record services outside HIPAA, including their
  ability to combine health information from multiple sources. Unauthorised
  disclosures can trigger duties, not only hacking. Assess whether the operator
  falls within the rule’s scope and FTC jurisdiction; an EU location does not
  settle this.
- **States:** assess laws for the users and data involved, including
  [Washington’s My Health My Data Act](https://www.atg.wa.gov/protecting-washingtonians-personal-health-data-and-privacy),
  [California’s CCPA](https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa) and consumer-health-data laws
  in [Nevada](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-603A.html#NRS603ASec400) and
  [Connecticut](https://portal.ct.gov/ag/sections/privacy/the-connecticut-data-privacy-act),
  with their respective thresholds and exemptions. Health/genetic privacy and
  breach laws may add duties. GDPR or HIPAA compliance does not settle coverage.

Operators must identify themselves, data uses, recipients, retention and rights
in a service-specific notice. Assess consent, deletion, research reuse, advertising
and transfers to third-party AI or analytics services. Establish incident-response
and notification processes; make only privacy promises the service implements.

## Research and responsibility

Operators must determine whether the [Common Rule](https://www.hhs.gov/ohrp/regulations-and-policy/regulations/common-rule/index.html),
FDA human-subject requirements or institutional policies apply. Obtain required
review and consent, or document an applicable exemption or waiver. A research-only
label does not establish one. Assess additional obligations before including
children or returning individual genetic interpretations.

See [warranty and responsibility](INTENDED-USE.md#warranty-and-responsibility).
